## Appointment-setting call script (verbatim)

Objective: get qualified, serious people on the calendar so the closer closes in office or by phone.

**Role boundary, stated as non-negotiable:** *"VA is NOT closing. VA is NOT negotiating. VA is NOT over-explaining."*

Five jobs only: call pre-screened leads, confirm fit and affordability, build interest, book the appointment, set expectations so the customer shows up.

**The flow, word for word:**

1. **Opening** — *"Hi, is this [Name]? This is [VA] calling from TMMT Rentals. You recently inquired about renting a vehicle — is now a good time to talk for 2 minutes?"* If no → reschedule, do not push.
2. **Hook** — *"Perfect. We specialize in long-term rentals for people who need reliable vehicles without dealership headaches."*
3. **Qualification checklist, in this fixed order:**
   - Term: *"How long are you looking to rent for — short-term or at least a few weeks/months?"* Ideal is 30+ days.
   - Insurance: *"Do you currently have full-coverage insurance or would you need in-house options?"* No insurance = flag, continue.
   - Use case: *"Is this vehicle for personal use, work, or rideshare?"*
   - Start date: *"When are you looking to get into a vehicle?"*
   - **Budget reality check:** *"Just to be transparent, our rentals typically start around $300 per week depending on the vehicle. Does that fit what you're looking for?"* — **If they hesitate → do NOT negotiate. Just note the response.**
4. **Position the closer** — *"Based on what you told me, it sounds like a good fit. The next step is a quick appointment with our manager to go over available vehicles and finalize everything."*
5. **Two-option close** — *"We do these by appointment only so everything is ready when you arrive. Would [Option A] or [Option B] work better for you?"*
6. **Show-up reinforcement** — *"Please bring: valid driver's license, proof of insurance, payment method. If anything changes, let us know in advance — appointments are reserved for serious renters."*

**Disqualify (mark low priority, do NOT argue, do NOT sell):** can't afford base pricing · needs daily-only · no licence · wants "cheap" or "deals" · won't commit to an appointment.

**Call notes required every time:** term, insurance Y/N, interest type, budget comfort Y/N, appointment booked Y/N, red flags.

**The heuristic:** *"If the VA can't confidently book the appointment in under 7 minutes → they're talking too much."*

Allowed language is deliberately narrow: *"We offer reliable daily-use vehicles like sedans and SUVs, as well as premium options depending on availability."* No discounts, no promises on specific models, no payment plans.

## Collections ladder (verbatim scripts)

**Timing rule:** *"Please only send messages in the mornings to people who missed a payment the day before, unless you are sending a message about receiving payments."*

**Payment received, always:** *"Thank you, we've received your payment of $___, your current balance is now $___"*

**Stage 1 — neutral notice.** *"Good morning, [Name] — our records show we did not receive your weekly payment due yesterday ([Date]). Please submit the missed payment as soon as possible to keep your account current and avoid delinquency. If you've already made the payment, please reply with a screenshot or confirmation number so we can update our records."*

**Stage 2 — the hardship door.** *"Hello [Name], thank you for the update. To keep your account in good standing, we can approve a 3-day payment of $[Amount] due today. You will then have an additional 48 hours to pay the remaining balance of $[Remaining]. Please confirm that you agree to this plan, and we will send an addendum for your signature."*

> **Every concession is papered with a signed addendum.** That is the mechanic worth keeping.

**Stage 3 — courtesy window with a named consequence.** *"…we are allowing 48 hours to submit the payment and bring your account current. If we do not hear from you and payment is not received within this timeframe, your account will be considered delinquent and will be escalated to our Fleet Department for repossession."*

**Stage 4 — escalation.** *"…your account has been sent over to our fleet department for repossession. They will be in contact with you to give you their time of arrival. Please remove all personal belongings from the vehicle and return it clean and in the same condition as received. If you would like to return the vehicle to the office please let us know."*

**Stage 5 — partial payer.** *"Although we appreciate you making some payments, we want to make sure we get your balance down so please make minimum $___ payments daily until your balance is paid off."*

Tighter tracks exist for 3-day customers (50% today, remainder in 24 hours, 24-hour courtesy window) and daily customers (50% now, remainder by 10PM).

**The judgement encoded here:** acknowledge partial payment before applying pressure · always offer a structured split before threatening · put every plan in writing · escalate to a *named department*, never a personal threat · state the consequence plainly and once per stage.

## The 30-minute strategy call

- **0–10 min DISCOVERY** — *"Understand where they are, what they want, and what's in the way. Listen more than talk."*
- **10–20 min PRESENTATION** — *"Show them specifically how your system solves their stated problem. Use their words back."*
- **20–25 min CLOSE** — ***"Present the offer. One offer only. Give them the price. Stop talking."***
- **25–30 min OBJECTIONS** — ***"Don't negotiate price — offer clarity."***

**Nurture timing:** *"Most closes happen at days 14, 30, and 90."*
**Retention:** Day 7 / 14 / 30 check-in calls — *"reduces churn by 40%+."* Monthly "Value Drop" — a new template, script or tool added every month. Public milestone recognition. One live session per month.

## Executive VA performance system

Framing: *"At TMMT Rentals, we do not hire assistants. We build operators, leaders, and executives."* Three jobs: protect company money, solve problems without asking, reduce the owner's workload.

**Daily Executive Report — a hard gate.** No report = automatic failure for the day. Late = failure for the day. Five required sections:

1. **Money I Protected Today** — collected, saved, or loss prevented. If $0, explain why.
2. **Problems I Solved Without Asking** — what happened, what I decided, what I did.
3. **Problems or Risks I Identified.**
4. **One Improvement I Made Today.**
5. **Tomorrow's Action Plan.**

*"Fluff answers are unacceptable."*

**Daily score out of 100:** money protected +25 · decisions made independently +20 · errors caused −20 · improvements suggested +15 · clarity, reliability, professionalism +20. Bands: 80–100 strong · 70–79 needs improvement · below 70 once = warning · twice = removal.

**The autonomy rule, verbatim:** ❌ *"What should I do?"* ✅ *"Here's what I did and why."* — *"If you wait instead of deciding, you are failing."*

**The money rule:** *"If you cannot clearly explain how your work connects to money, you are at risk."* Every role must collect money, protect money, or prevent losses.

**System ownership:** each VA owns one system. By Day 30 they must show improvement, clear documentation, and reduced owner involvement.

## Fleet lifecycle SOP — nine stages

1. **Intake** — on arrival log VIN, make/model/year, plate, mileage, arrival date, condition notes, photos (front, rear, sides, interior). Assign a Fleet ID.
2. **Initial inspection** — before it can be rented: exterior damage, interior, tyres, lights, electronics, fluids, brakes. Anything needing repair → "Maintenance Hold."
3. **Preparation** — clean, fuel/charge, confirm registration and insurance, emergency kit inside, status "Ready / Available."
4. **Maintenance** — oil every 3,000–5,000 miles, tyre rotation every 5,000–7,500, brakes every 10,000, annual state inspection. Log date, mileage, provider.
5. **Checkout** — verify licence and insurance, collect payment/deposit, record mileage and fuel, **walk-around with the customer**, sign agreement, status "Rented Out."
6. **Return** — inspect, record mileage and fuel, photograph new damage, status "Cleaning / Inspection."
7. **Incident & damage** — customer notifies immediately; collect photos, police report if applicable, customer statement.
8. **Emergencies** — driver safety first, emergency services, notify management, arrange roadside/tow, document time, location, details.
9. **Records retained a minimum of 3 years.**

## Deal routing — which contract, what percentage

1. **Partner brings their own car** → JV Partner Agreement v3. Fee tiers: 10% (≤3 cars) · 20% (≤5) · 30% (≤10) · 40% full-service · 50% premium done-for-you. **"The MORE we do for them, the higher our fee. Match the % to the real work."**
2. **Someone drives our car to earn** → Operator/Driver Profit-Share. Operator keeps up to 50% of **net**. *"Net = earnings minus agreed costs (fuel, app fees, insurance portion, maintenance). No hidden cuts."*
3. **Customer renting** → Vehicle Ijārah lease. Set rent + ʿarbūn deposit. **No interest, no kept late fees, notice before repossession.**
4. **Renting to own** → the lease **plus a separate Ownership-Transfer Promise.** *"Rent is for use; ownership passes separately. Never bake ownership into rent."*
5. **Credit/funding referral** → honest terms only. **"NEVER promise a score, a funding amount, or 'passive income.' Ranges only."**
6. **Investor or buyer** → Investor Room. *"Don't share internal financials loose."*

Every deal: honest terms before anyone signs · no riba · mercy in genuine hardship · set the state · **higher % means more service from us, not just taking more** · if unsure, ask.

## The AI operating loop

Goal: *"remove you as the daily planning bottleneck."*

1. **Morning** — the system reads yesterday's recap, open jobs, vehicle status and customer promises, and produces today's plan.
2. **Through the day** — operators reply in plain language (`START 14`, `BLOCKED 14 no parts`, `END 14`, or a voice note). The system files it and **replies in friendly plain language**: *"Got it — job 14 done. 2 still open for you."*
   > The insight: ***"Loose input, clear confirmation. That's what makes the team actually use it."***
3. **Relay** — VAs ask the system first instead of the owner. *"Most questions die here."*
4. **Escalation** — only the five conditions above reach the owner.
5. **Evening** — the recap becomes tomorrow's plan, including **who failed to report.** *"The recap IS tomorrow morning's plan — same artifact, surfaced twice."*

Build order: ship the check-in loop **first** — *"This is the fuel; nothing else works without it"* — then the recap, then the morning plan, then the escalation filter last.

## Persuasion pattern: mandate framed as benefit

From the partner roadside-coverage email. The structure is reusable for any requirement imposed on a partner at their own cost.

- **Lead with the business reason, before the ask** — *"To ensure our fleet maintains maximum uptime and avoids costly delays…"*
- **Justify each requirement with named benefits**, not assertion — towing mileage *"prevents storage fees + relocation costs"*; *"works even if the driver isn't the car owner — great for rentals."*
- **Close by tying it to the partner's own money** — *"We eliminate downtime and protect your vehicle from extended impound/storage scenarios — keeping the car on the road making money."*
- **Then a concrete compliance step** (send screenshot confirmation) **plus an offer of help.**
