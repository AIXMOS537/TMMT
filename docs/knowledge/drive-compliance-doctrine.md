## The Credit-to-Keys Ecosystem — design and guardrails

The owner's own strategy document. Designs a five-layer membership (learn → fix credit → complete tasks → qualify → unlock a car or home) and maps regulation onto each layer.

Its thesis, verbatim: *"three of your design choices sit on top of the most heavily regulated activities in the country: credit repair, multi-level payouts, and promising assets (cars/homes). The good news is each has a clean, legal version."*

### Layer 1 — Education is the safe foundation

Selling financial education (courses, coaching, modules) may be charged for freely, including on monthly membership. Strategic instruction: lead with this, because *"the more your ecosystem is genuinely an education-and-community business, the safer everything else is."*

### Layer 2 — Credit repair under CROA. Four hard constraints

1. **No upfront fees.** No payment may be collected for credit-repair work until the promised work is *fully completed*. A monthly membership that includes "we fix your credit," charged before the work is done, is a CROA violation — *"a rule that breaks most beginner business models."*
2. **Written, dated contract before any work begins**, stating services, total cost, timeframe, any guarantees, and the customer's **right to cancel within 3 days** with no penalty.
3. **Mandatory disclosure** that the consumer has the right to fix their own credit themselves and to dispute items directly.
4. **Accuracy limit.** You cannot promise to remove information that is **accurate and timely**. Only inaccurate or unverifiable items may be challenged.

### The key strategic judgement in the whole corpus

*"Separate education from done-for-you repair. Many successful operators teach members to do it themselves — that sidesteps most of CROA entirely while still delivering the result."*

Education = chargeable normally. Done-for-you repair = not billable until complete.

### Layer 4 — The qualification gate

The structural protection against asset-promise liability: a member unlocks a car or home because **a real lender or landlord approved them**, not because they paid. *"Build the unlock around genuine third-party approval, and you're delivering a real outcome instead of an empty promise."*

### Layer 5 — Never guarantee the asset

*"'We help you qualify' is true and legal. 'Join and get a car' is a promise you can't keep for everyone and is exactly the kind of claim that draws enforcement."*

### The network / MLM layer — highest risk in the design

Legal only if participants are paid primarily for **real product sales to real end customers**, not recruitment. Once money comes mainly from people paying to join and recruiting others, the FTC treats it as an **illegal pyramid scheme regardless of whether a product exists**, and brings cases against individual participants, not only companies.

Five survival rules:
1. Pay on real sales, never a signup bounty.
2. Prefer a **one-time referral bonus or discount** over an ongoing multi-level chain — *"if you can hit your goals with that, take it."*
3. No income promises, ever. Publish an income-disclosure statement if earnings are mentioned at all.
4. Never require members to buy inventory or pay to "qualify" to earn — classic pyramid red flag.
5. Lawyer review of the compensation plan specifically — *"the one piece I'd never launch without legal sign-off."*

### Build order

Education layer first → qualification and unlock path with written partner agreements → **credit help last and carefully, ideally as teach-them-to-do-it**; if done-for-you, build the CROA contract, 3-day cancel, disclosures and no-advance-fee billing *before taking a dollar* → design network comp with a lawyer, starting at one-time referral bonuses → scrub all marketing for guarantees and income claims before anything goes public.

Summary as written: *"Build it as an education and community business that helps people genuinely qualify for cars and homes, pays its network on real sales, and treats credit repair under CROA rules… Skip it, and the multi-level + credit-repair + asset-promise combination is the textbook profile regulators shut down."*

## Approved credit / funding marketing language

The compliant replacement script. Opens by naming why the old one failed: *"Earn $800-$1,000/month on autopilot," "without lifting a finger," "boost their scores," "secure $50K+ funding"* are *"promises you can't truthfully guarantee (dishonest = no barakah) AND likely illegal under US credit-repair law (CROA) and FTC rules."*

**The compliant partner offer, reusable:**

> "Want to add a credit & funding service to what you already offer? We provide a done-for-you back office so you can refer clients and earn a share of the work we complete — without being a credit expert yourself. You refer clients who want help improving their credit profile and exploring funding options. Our team does the work, transparently, by the book. You earn a referral/partner share on completed, paid engagements. Earnings depend on how many clients you refer and what they sign up for — we don't promise a fixed amount. What we will NOT do: promise a specific credit score, promise a specific funding amount, or promise guaranteed income. Results vary by each client's situation, and we follow all applicable laws."

**Five standing rules for all credit/funding messaging:**
1. Never guarantee a score, a funding amount, or income. Say "may," "could," "depends," with ranges.
2. No "passive / autopilot / without lifting a finger" language.
3. Disclose that results vary and that fees and terms apply.
4. Follow CROA: no charging for credit repair before services are performed; give required disclosures.
5. When unsure, ask management before sending. Honesty first — always.

Note the comp-plan design embedded in rule form: partner share pays on **completed, paid engagements** — the CROA no-advance-fee rule expressed as compensation timing.

## Adverse-action and screening language

From the background-check SOP. Directly transferable to credit and funding intake.

Staff must **never** say: *"You failed a background check," "You have a criminal record,"* or *"[the vendor] says you did XYZ."*

Required substitute phrasing: **"Based on internal risk guidelines, we're unable to move forward at this time."**

Vendor caveat, stated in the SOP itself: the screening tool *"is NOT an official consumer reporting agency (CRA) — it is for informational screening only. Final adverse actions should be based on rental policy + insurance eligibility, not the report alone."*

Decision discipline: *"employees do not make moral judgments — decisions are rule-based only."* Ambiguous cases — name similarity, incomplete info, old minor records — are **escalated, never decided** by staff.

Data handling: **"Never screenshot or download reports. Never email reports externally."**

Documentation per check: date, tool used, match confidence (High/Medium/Low), Pass/Fail/Escalated, employee initials.

## Deal routing — which contract for which shape

- Partner brings their own car → JV Partner Agreement v3, fee tiered 10% (≤3 cars) / 20% (≤5) / 30% (≤10) / 40% full-service / 50% premium. Principle: **"higher % = more service from us."**
- Someone drives our car for income → Operator/Driver profit-share, operator keeps up to 50% of **net** (earnings minus agreed costs).
- Customer renting → Vehicle Ijārah lease v3. *"Set rent + 'arboun deposit. No interest, no kept late fees, notice before repo."*
- Customer renting **to own** → the lease **plus a separate Ownership-Transfer Promise**, two documents. *"Rent is for use; ownership passes separately (gift or nominal sale). Never bake ownership into rent."*
- **Credit/funding referral → "HONEST terms only, no guarantees."**
- Investor/buyer → Investor Room, *"no loose financials."*

Footer rule on every deal: *"honest terms up front · no riba · mercy in hardship · set the state · ask if unsure."*

## Code of conduct — the values layer

Standard: *"honesty, mercy, and fairness — and we seek blessing (barakah), not just profit. A clean dollar beats ten with no blessing in it."*

- **Be trustworthy** — *"no fake numbers, no fake testimonials, no promises we can't keep ('guaranteed income,' 'guaranteed score' — never)."* Disclose real terms before anyone signs. *"If you don't know, say 'let me find out' — never make something up to close a deal."*
- **Be merciful when people owe us** — *"Collect firmly but never cruelly. No threats, no insults, no calling at bad hours."* **Always offer a hardship door** — job loss, illness, family emergency get a fair worked-out plan. Repossession is a last resort, with notice and respect, *"never to humiliate."*
- **Be fair in money** — no interest/riba; charge what was quoted; apply payments honestly; refund what's owed; partners get the agreed split on time.
- **Serve the person, not just the sale** — *"Many customers are rebuilding their lives. Help them get on their feet — don't trap them."*
- **Escalation** — *"If a script, fee, or request feels dishonest or harsh, flag it to management before you act. Doing right is never the wrong call here."*

## Riba and contract-fairness flags

Raised for a scholar and an attorney, not yet answered:

- Lease payment allocation applying payments to *"late fees, then accrued interest, then principal"* — flagged as riba; remove interest entirely and handle overdue amounts without it.
- Late fees kept as company profit — *"Is a late fee permissible at all? Many scholars allow it ONLY if donated to charity, not kept as income."*
- Lease-to-own structure — is it a valid *ijārah muntahia bittamleek*, with ownership transfer as a **separate gift or sale rather than financing baked into rent**.
- Early termination forfeiting remaining payments plus two months' fee — *"Can we charge for time/use not yet taken?"*
- Insurance section contradicts itself on who insures — unclear terms are *gharar* as well as legal risk.
- Repossession *"without prior notice"* — flagged as ethically and often legally wrong; check state repossession-notice law.
- Governing law written as a **county** rather than a state.

Routing rule: Islamic-finance questions to a scholar familiar with AAOIFI; US law questions to a business attorney licensed in the operating state.

## Collections ladder

Timing rule: *"Please only send messages in the mornings to people who missed a payment the day before."*

Five stages, escalating, with the same discipline at each — factual, no threats, consequence stated plainly and once:

1. **Neutral notice** — payment due yesterday not received. *"If you've already made the payment, please reply with a screenshot or confirmation number so we can update our records."*
2. **The hardship door** — a concrete structured plan offered, always closing with *"Please confirm that you agree to this plan, and we will send an addendum for your signature."* **Every concession is papered.**
3. **Courtesy window with a named consequence** — 48 or 24 hours, after which the account escalates.
4. **Escalation notice** — states the account has gone to fleet; instructions to remove belongings and return the vehicle clean; option to return it to the office instead.
5. **Partial-payer script** — acknowledges payments made, then requires minimum daily payments until current.

Escalation authority: **GREEN** (VAs) small issues and reminders · **YELLOW** (Secretary) complaints, vehicle issues, late payments, tickets · **RED** (Owner only) police, accidents, repossession approval, theft, major money issues.

## Sales-call judgement

**30-minute strategy call:** 0–10 min Discovery (*"Listen more than talk"*) · 10–20 min Presentation (*"Use their words back"*) · 20–25 min Close (**"One offer only. Give them the price. Stop talking."**) · 25–30 min Objections (**"Don't negotiate price — offer clarity"**).

**Appointment-setting SOP.** Role boundary: *"VA is NOT closing. VA is NOT negotiating. VA is NOT over-explaining."*

Budget reality check stated transparently up front: *"Just to be transparent, our rentals typically start around $X depending on the vehicle. Does that fit what you're looking for?"* — with the instruction **"If they hesitate → do NOT negotiate. Just note response."**

Two-option appointment lock: *"Would [Option A] or [Option B] work better?"*

The heuristic worth keeping: **"If the VA can't confidently book the appointment in under 7 minutes → they're talking too much."**

Nurture timing: *"Most closes happen at days 14, 30, and 90."* Retention: Day 7 / 14 / 30 check-in calls.
