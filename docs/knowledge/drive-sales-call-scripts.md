## The main consultative call — 14 parts

A 45-minute high-ticket structure. The most complete sales asset found anywhere in the estate.

### Before the call

- Quiet room, no interruptions
- Zoom on a computer with headphones, **so hands are free to take notes and gesture** rather than holding a phone
- **Record the call**
- Pen and notepad in front of you
- **"Release all attachment to the 'YES' and put all attachment onto 'Accurate Diagnosis'"**
- Lose the emotional baggage from the day or previous calls — be tranquil and focused
- Start the very moment the clock hits the minute

> That fifth line is the whole philosophy of the script in one sentence, and it is the part
> worth keeping if everything else is discarded: **the goal of the call is a correct diagnosis,
> not a sale.** Everything downstream follows from getting that right.

### Part 1 — Small talk
*"Hey [Name], how are you? Where are you calling from today?"*
Then: *"Because we only have 45 minutes, is it alright if we get straight into it?"*

### Part 2 — State the agenda and take the lead
*"How this call will go is I'll start off by asking you some questions about where you are…
Then if it sounds like I can help and we're a good fit, I'll explain what I have to offer and
how that works. Then at the end you can make a decision whether you want to be part of it or not."*

Wait for the acknowledgement before moving on. **The permission is the lead.**

### Part 3 — Why are they here
*"Tell me what motivated you to take the time out of your day and schedule a call with me?"*

Probing questions if the first answer is thin:
- *"What do you mean by…?"*
- *"Tell me more about that."*
- *"Why do you think this problem exists?"*
- *"What else have you tried to do to fix this?"*
- *"How long have you been dealing with this?"*

### Part 4 — Current situation
Working or studying · what have they started or are thinking of starting · how is it going ·
what makes them want to start a business while already working.

### Part 5 — Point A, the present, with a number
- *"How much are you currently making from your work/business?"*
- *"Do you like what you are doing?"*
- *"Do you see yourself having this type of credit score for the next 10 years?"*
- *"Is this giving you the freedom you want, or are you hoping for something else?"*

**"You must get a specific dollar figure per month before moving forward."**

### Part 6 — Point B, the target, with a number
*"Where do you want to grow this in the next 12 months?"* — again, a specific monthly figure.

Then: *"What is your motivation for getting to $XX?"* · *"How would things be different?"* ·
*"Would getting to $XX have an impact on other areas of your life?"*

Instruction: support the vision, spend time defining it. *"If they are aiming too low, help them
aim higher and widen what's possible for them beyond what they might see for themselves."*

### Part 7 — Releasing control, and self-admission

*"So you're currently making $A a month and you want to get to $B. Tell me what's stopping you
from achieving that on your own?"*

**Listen for the three statements before moving forward:**
1. Inability to do it on their own
2. Wanting to do it faster
3. Wanting to follow a proven system, guided by somebody who has actually done it

> This is the hinge of the whole call. If none of the three appears, the prospect has not
> admitted they need help — and the offer that follows will be a push rather than an answer.

### Part 8 — Commitment
- *"Why not just stay where you are?"*
- *"Is not having this affecting any other areas of your life?"*
- *"When are you wanting to fix this?"*
- *"How much time can you dedicate to fixing this problem?"*
- *"Is this something you want to fix right now, or do you want to wait?"*
- *"How committed are you to make this happen?"*

⚠️ The script notes these questions surface personal drivers — divorce, single parenthood,
hardship. **Handle that with the standard the code of conduct already sets:** *"Serve the person,
not just the sale. Many customers are rebuilding their lives. Help them get on their feet —
don't trap them."* Hearing a hardship is a reason to be careful about what you sell, not a
lever to press.

## The mentorship call — short version

A compressed variant of the same shape, for a single-offer call:

1. Small talk · 2. Agenda · 3. *"What made you book this call today?"* · 4. Current situation —
*"Have you tried car rentals yet or is this brand new?"* · 5. Point A — *"What's missing right
now? Do you feel like you have a clear roadmap?"* · 6. Point B · 7. Self-admission — listen for
*"I don't know where to start" · "I need a faster path" · "I want someone who's done it to guide
me"* · 8. Commitment — *"Why not just stay where you are?"* · 9. Position the offer ·
10. Explain it · 11. Deliver the price.

Offer framing used: *"One-on-one mentorship, weekly calls, full templates, DM access, support
from A to Z."*

## The generic consultative template

A neutral structure for a cold or semi-warm call, useful where the 14-part version is too long:

1. **Greeting + rapport** — reference something specific and true about them, not flattery
2. **Identify the pain point** — *"Many businesses in [industry] struggle with [pain]. Is that
   something you've experienced?"* — then **listen and validate** before responding
3. **Present the solution** with a concrete example of it working for someone comparable
4. **Build trust** with a testimonial or case study carrying a measurable outcome
5. **Handle objections by pre-empting them** — *"I know you might be wondering about the cost…
   is that something you'd like to discuss?"*
6. **Close on a specific next step** with a confirmed date and time
7. **Follow up** with an email recapping the conversation and the next step

| Cold or generic | Consultative template | `gdrive` account |
| Structure and timing | 30-minute strategy call · closes at days 14, 30, 90 | 50-App framework |

**The setter never closes and the closer never sets.** That boundary is stated explicitly in the
setter SOP — *"VA is NOT closing. VA is NOT negotiating."* — and the 14-part script assumes it,
opening with a prospect who has already been qualified and has an application on file.

## Compliance note for anything credit-adjacent

Part 5 asks *"Do you see yourself having this type of credit score for the next 10 years?"*,
which puts this script directly in front of credit and funding prospects.

The five standing rules apply to every word said on those calls: **never guarantee a score, a
funding amount, or income** · no *passive / autopilot / without lifting a finger* language ·
disclose that results vary and fees apply · follow CROA — no charging for credit repair before
services are performed · when unsure, ask before sending.

The script's own emotional-driver instruction and those rules point the same way: **get the
diagnosis right, and only sell what you can actually deliver.**
